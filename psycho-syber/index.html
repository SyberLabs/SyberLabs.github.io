    The game

    PSYCHO SYBER is a side-scrolling stealth game that runs in this page. It has five stages. Each stage stands for one project and takes its rule from what that project does.

    It is set in an invented ancient Paracas, on the south coast of Peru. Long-skulled Visitors live among the people and run the Works, where a machine approves which births may go ahead. You play a weaver who stole one of their circlets.

    Two forms

    Shift changes your form. The picture, the music and what you can do change with it.

    SYBER
    The weaver, wearing the circlet. Throws glass, swings on a knotted cord and runs the check with E. On a Visitor machine the check takes the machine over. On stone it marks each step proven, stale or broken. On a lot in the last stage it reads the record.
    PSYCHO
    The jaguar. It runs faster, climbs stone, fits through low tunnels and pounces. E is a roar that slows everything else for about three seconds. It stands on spirit paths the weaver falls through. It sees which source feeds each gate and which villagers are Visitors.

    Five stages, five projects

    1. 01

      THE STEPS · Attest

      A stair where some steps are stale and some are missing. The check marks each one. Attest runs every step in your docs and marks the ones that fail.

    2. 02

      THE RISING · RISE

      A climb on words that light one at a time. The roar slows them. RISE shows text at a pace you set, with sound and moving visuals.

    3. 03

      THE LINES · FLYSPACE

      Gates on the desert floor, each fed by a source somewhere else. The jaguar sees the line between them. FLYSPACE shows which data sources fed each AI answer.

    4. 04

      THE WORKS · SyberWork

      The Visitors' factory, with stations named Propose, Check, Review, Approve and Publish. At Approve a machine decides alone until you cut its three sources. SyberWork tests every AI-written code change before a person approves it.

    5. 05

      THE RECORD · Lacuna Institute

      A viewing hall before a sale. Seven lots stand on plinths. E reads the record of a lot, and five of the records have a gap. You deliver that evidence at the far door before the sale timer runs out. Nothing is taken and no one is fought. Lacuna Institute describes its work as “Finding the history that fell out of the record.”

    The 21 Atlas marks

    The last stage holds 21 marks, one for each site in the Lacuna Atlas. A mark carries the name of its site and links to that entry. The game says nothing else about any site. Each Atlas entry has sections headed “What's known”, “What's missing”, “The open question” and “Sources”.

    What is real

    The story is invented: the Visitors, the Works, the sale and every lot in it. No person, dealer, auction house or museum in the game is real.

    Paracas is real. The Lacuna Atlas entry on it begins: “The elongated skulls of Paracas are real. So is the paperwork gap around where some of the burials went.” The same entry says: “Shaping an infant's skull with boards and bindings was a deliberate cultural practice, not a mystery of biology.”

    Limits

    • Keyboard and mouse only. Touch controls are not built yet.
    • Checked in Chrome on a desktop. Firefox and Safari have not been checked.
    • Each stage has a scripted run that plays it to the exit. The runs cover one route through each stage.
    • Progress is kept in this browser's storage. Clearing site data resets it.
    • All sound is synthesised in the browser. M switches it off.
