      SyberLabs / Game
      
        
      Six stages built from SyberLabs mechanics.
      
        
      PSYCHO SYBER is a browser stealth game set in a fictionalized ancient Paracas. Each of the first five stages translates a SyberLabs project into play rules; the sixth has no exit.
      
        
      You play a noble weaver who stole a Visitor circlet, shadowed by a jaguar spirit, confronting the Patron’s automated Works, the viewing-hall auction, and the machine called the Thousand Eyes.
      
      
      

      
      
        
      Weaver or jaguar.
      
        
      Press Shift (or Q) to shift forms, changing visuals, music, and abilities while leaving a 3-second decoy double (8-second cooldown) that distracts watching Visitors.
      
        
      Every solvable stage can be beaten purely as the weaver via terminals, doors, and stairs; the jaguar offers faster, riskier routes.
      
        
      
          
      SYBER (The Weaver)
      Throws glass distractions, swings by knotted cord, and triggers E to run checks: hijacking Visitor machines, flagging proven, stale, or broken stone steps, and auditing provenance records.
      
          
      PSYCHO (The Jaguar)
      Faster movement, wall climbs, low tunnels, spirit paths, and pounce attacks that shatter power sources (triggering alarms). E unleashes a mountain-echoed roar that brings rain and slows enemies for ~3 seconds. Reveals hidden source wires, disguised Visitors, and broken provenance lines.
      
        
      
      
      

      
      
        
      Six stages
      
        
        
          
      1. 01
        THE STEPS · Attest
        Navigate stairs with stale and missing steps; run checks to mark each one. Mirrors Attest’s executable documentation testing.
        2. 
          
      2. 02
        MACHU PICCHU · RISE
        Ascend ruins across timed illuminated words whose pace you control via roars or pace-setters (with an alternate stone stair). Mirrors RISE’s paced reading.
        3. 
          
      3. 03
        THE LINES · FLYSPACE
        Desert gates fed by remote sources; jaguar vision reveals every hidden connection. Mirrors FLYSPACE’s source lineage.
        4. 
          
      4. 04
        THE WORKS · SyberWork
        Stations named Propose, Check, Review, Approve, and Publish. Cut three automated feeds at Approve to restore human sign-off. Mirrors SyberWork.
        5. 
          
      5. 05
        THE RECORD · Lacuna Institute
        Inspect seven auction lots before the clock expires, identify five broken records, and deliver the evidence without combat or theft. Mirrors Lacuna’s provenance audits (“Mapping the history missing from the record.”).
        6. 
          
      6. 06
        THE REAL BATTLE · SyberLabs
        A single room with no exit against the Thousand Eyes, which anticipates your path, logs every move, and counts down to a verdict. Every Atlas mark found disables a lens and buys one extra second.
        7. 
        
      
      
      

      
      
        
      The 21 Atlas marks
      
        
      21 collectible marks across stages 1 to 5 (3 per stages 1–4, 9 in stage 5) link directly to the 21 Lacuna Atlas entries, each structured under “What is known”, “What is missing”, “Open question”, and “Sources”.
      
        
      Progress persists locally in browser storage; completed stages unlock direct replay with mark counters. In stage 6, each mark darkens a lens and delays the verdict by one second.

      Music
      
        
      Synthesized live in-browser (56–96 bpm, A minor pentatonic over a sustained fifth drone). Crossfades between loom/stone percussion (Weaver) and twin pipes, heartbeat drum, rattles, and conch (Jaguar). Stage 5 drops the drum; stage 6 accelerates toward the verdict.
      
        
      
      

      
      
        
      Fiction vs. history
      
        
      The Visitors, Patron, Works, auction, and Thousand Eyes are fictional; no actual dealer, museum, or company appears (one hidden scene briefly features three SyberLabs team members by nickname).
      
        
      Paracas cranial shaping and provenance gaps are documented history. The Lacuna Atlas entry notes: “The elongated skulls of Paracas are real. So is the paperwork gap around where some of the burials went.” and “Shaping an infant's skull with boards and bindings was a deliberate cultural practice, not a mystery of biology.”
      
        
      Paracas in the Lacuna Atlas · The Paracas bundles dataset
      
        
      Machu Picchu (built mid-1400s for Inka emperor Pachacuti) is historically much later than Paracas, placed along the same road for fiction. Smarthistory: Machu Picchu
      
      
      

      
      
        
      Cultural sources
      
        
      The jaguar mechanics freely blend distinct ancient American traditions from different peoples and eras:
      
        
        
          
      • Half-form & standing stones. Olmec were-jaguar carvings from Mexico (cleft head, rain-deity hypotheses). Wikipedia: Werejaguar
        • 
          
      • Decoy double. Mesoamerican nagual animal doubles. Britannica: nagual
        • 
          
      • Mountain-echo roar. Aztec jaguar deity Tepeyollotl, tied to caves and echoes. Wikipedia: Tepeyollotl
        • 
          
      • Spirit paths. Maya night-sun jaguar traversing the underworld. Wikipedia: Maya jaguar gods
        • 
          
      • The drink. Chavín de Huántar Circular Plaza carvings holding San Pedro cactus. Stanford: Chavín de Huántar
        • 
        
      
      
      

      
      
        
      Limits
      
        
        
          
      • Verified in desktop Chrome and mobile emulation (opened once on iPhone; full touch playthrough and Firefox/Safari remain untested).
        • 
          
      • Upright phone mode shrinks stage 6 text; mobile text speed locks at 220 wpm.
        • 
          
      • iOS audio attempts playback past the mute switch (unverified on hardware; silenced pre-iOS 16.4).
        • 
          
      • CI verifies all five exitable stages via automated Weaver-only and Jaguar scripts plus stage 6’s no-exit ending.
        • 
          
      • The ending displays your local device clock and briefly updates the tab title (Esc/Enter skips; honors reduced motion). Zero data leaves your device; progress saves locally in browser storage.
        • 
          
      • Synthesized audio levels were calibrated via Chrome measurements without human listening calibration.
