      SyberLabs / Game
      
        
      The game
      
        
      PSYCHO SYBER is a side-scrolling stealth game that runs in this page. It has six stages. Each of the first five stands for one project and takes its rule from what that project does. The sixth has no exit.
      
        
      It is set in an invented ancient Paracas, on the south coast of Peru. In this story a long head marks a noble house, and the Visitors who live among the people wear the same shape. The Visitors run the Works, where a machine approves which births may go ahead. The Patron, a Visitor in gold, owns the Works, the sale in the fifth stage and the machine in the sixth. You play a noble weaver who stole one of their circlets and drank what the old ones drank. A jaguar came with you.
      
        
      THE ANOMALOUS SYSTEM is a seventh scene, started from its own button on the title. It is not one of the six stages and does not change your progress through them.
      
        
      PSYCHO SYBER: ARENA is a second game on its own page: a first-person arena in 3D with the same two forms, for one player or for two players in two browsers.
      
      
      

      
      
        
      Two forms
      
        
      Shift changes your form. The picture, the music and what you can do change with it. For a moment at each change you are both: a person's body under the jaguar's head. The form you leave stays behind as a double for five seconds, at most once every eight. A warden within 15 tiles that has a clear line to the double turns to it and watches it for as long as it stands, and a line is drawn from the warden to the double. The line is drawn from a warden dressed as a villager too. While it watches, it does not fire and it does not see you. The first stage puts a warden on a beam near the start, with a standing stone where it cannot see you, so the double can be tried there.
      
        
      The hint on screen is written for the form you are in. At the first wall the weaver is told about the ring and the cord, and the jaguar about the climb.
      
        
      Each of the first five stages can be finished without taking the jaguar's form. Where the jaguar goes under a wall, up a cliff or across a path only it can stand on, the weaver has a terminal that opens a door, a stair, or a ladder of beams. The weaver's way takes longer.
      
        
      
          
      SYBER
      The weaver, wearing the circlet. Throws glass, swings on a knotted cord and runs the check with E. On a Visitor machine it turns a sentinel, shuts off an eye or source, opens a door, or sets the word pace. On stone it marks each step proven, stale or broken. On a lot in the fifth stage it reads the record.
      
          
      PSYCHO
      The jaguar. It runs faster, climbs stone, fits through low tunnels and pounces. E is a roar: the mountain echoes it three times, rain falls, nearby wardens and sentinels are stunned, and everything else slows for about three seconds. It stands on spirit paths the weaver falls through. It sees which source feeds each gate and which villagers are Visitors. A pounce breaks a source, and the alarm goes up when it does. In the fifth stage it sees each record as a line: a broken line is a gap. Its view ripples and its colours drift, except close around the jaguar, where the picture holds still. The jaguar is drawn with a dark edge, the tops it can land on nearby are edged in white, and marks rise up a stone face it can climb.
      
        
      
      
      

      
      
        
      Six stages
      
        
        
          
      1. 01
        THE STEPS · Attest
        A stair where some steps are stale and some are missing. The check marks each one. Attest runs every step in your docs and marks the ones that fail.
        2. 
          
      2. 02
        MACHU PICCHU · RISE
        A climb through Inca ruins on words that light one at a time. You set their pace: the jaguar's roar slows them for a moment, and a pace setter in the ravine slows them until you check it again. Parts one and three also have beam stairs for the weaver. RISE shows text at a pace you set, with sound and moving visuals.
        3. 
          
      3. 03
        THE LINES · FLYSPACE
        Gates on the desert floor, each fed by a source somewhere else. The jaguar sees the line between them. FLYSPACE shows which data sources fed each AI answer.
        4. 
          
      4. 04
        THE WORKS · SyberWork
        The Visitors' factory, with stations named Propose, Check, Review, Approve and Publish. At Approve a machine decides alone until you cut its three sources. SyberWork tests every AI-written code change before a person approves it.
        5. 
          
      5. 05
        THE RECORD · Lacuna Institute
        A viewing hall before a sale. Seven lots stand on plinths. E reads the record of a lot, and five of the records have a gap. You deliver that evidence at the far door before the sale timer runs out. Nothing is taken and no one is fought. Lacuna Institute describes its work as “Finding the history that fell out of the record.”
        6. 
          
      6. 06
        THE REAL BATTLE · SyberLabs
        One room with no exit, and a machine called the Thousand Eyes. It aims each shot at the place you are about to reach, lists what you do on the screen as you do it, and counts down to a verdict. Glass, the check, the roar and hiding do not stop it. The stage ends when the count reaches zero or your health runs out, and the ending follows. SyberLabs makes software for checking AI's work: each change is tested, and a person approves it.
        7. 
        
      
        
      THE ANOMALOUS SYSTEM
      
        
      The scene is set inside the Thousand Eyes, drawn as an operating system: a boot sector, a desktop of windows, a registry, a defragmenter, a firewall and a core. Its rules differ from the six stages in four ways.
      
        
        
          
      • Every change of form leaves a copy where you stood, with no eight-second wait. Up to five copies stand at once, each for 12 seconds.
        • 
          
      • A copy corrupts metal within a tile of it, and the corruption spreads through connected metal at five tiles a second. Corrupt metal takes the cord and the jaguar's claws. A gate or an eye set in a connected piece of metal goes dark when that whole piece is corrupt.
        • 
          
      • The scene opens with a panel of metal across the way and nothing hunting you. A copy left against the panel corrupts it, and you climb over. The system wakes once a copy has corrupted metal and you are past the panel. A cursor then hunts the nearest copy and deletes it; with no copy left, it comes for you unless you hide. The roar and thrown glass stop it for a moment.
        • 
          
      • When the system wakes, a scan line starts at the left edge and crosses the scene in four minutes. It cleans the metal it passes, moves twice as fast while the alarm is up, and sends you back to the last standing stone if it reaches you. The first copy in each piece of metal sets the line back by 12 tiles.
        • 
        
      
        
      The exit opens when the core is broken. The machine restores itself from a copy; the card shows the share of the metal that was corrupt when you left, and the title button shows your best share. While the scene is on, letters in its name and in this tab's title turn into figures as that share rises. With reduced motion set on your device they stay as they are.
      
      
      

      
      
        
      The 21 Atlas marks
      
        
      The first five stages hold 21 marks between them, one for each site in the Lacuna Atlas: three in each of the first four stages and nine in the fifth. Each mark carries the name of its site; the list below and the fifth stage's end card link to every entry. The game says nothing else about any site. Each Atlas entry has sections headed “What's known”, “What's missing”, “The open question” and “Sources”.
      
        
      The marks you find are kept in this browser. Once you have finished a stage, the title shows a numbered button for each stage you have reached, with the count of marks found in it, and each button starts that stage. In the last stage each mark found is a lens on the machine's wall that has gone dark, and the verdict comes one second later for each.

      On a phone or tablet
      
        
      Touch controls show while a stage is on, and the stage takes the whole screen. On a phone held upright the stage is drawn sideways along the long edge, which makes the picture about three times the size: turn the phone to play. This works whether or not the phone's rotation is locked. Pause has a button to play upright instead, and a button back to the title, where this page's header returns.
      
        
        
          
      • The stick on the left walks. Up reels in the cord; as the jaguar, push into stone and up to climb. Down lowers the cord, climbs down, hides, and with Jump drops through a beam.
        • 
          
      • Jump, Form and E are buttons on the right. Jump leaps off stone while climbing. E runs the check, or roars as the jaguar.
        • 
          
      • A tap on the picture throws glass, or pounces as the jaguar, toward the place you tapped.
        • 
          
      • Press and hold on a ring, a beam or stone to throw the cord. It stays tied when you lift your finger. A tap or Jump lets it go.
        • 
          
      • The note button switches the sound off and on.

      Music
      
        
      Music starts at your first tap or key. It is slow, at 56 to 96 beats a minute. One low note is held under everything with a fifth over it and no third, and over that a tune in A minor pentatonic moves in long notes. The jaguar hears the tune on two pipes tuned a few cents apart, over a drum that beats like a heart, seed rattles and a conch horn. The weaver hears it struck on stone, over the clack of a loom. Changing form crossfades between the two without restarting the tune. Each stage sets its own pitch and pace, the fifth stage has no drum, and in the last stage the pace rises as the verdict nears.
      
        
      
      

      
      
        
      What is real
      
        
      The story is invented: the Visitors, the Patron, the Works, the sale, every lot in it and the Thousand Eyes. No dealer, auction house, museum or company in the game exists. The story's people are invented too, with one exception: a hidden scene in which three members of SyberLabs appear by first name or nickname and role. The operating system in THE ANOMALOUS SYSTEM is invented and stands for no product.
      
        
      Paracas is real. The Lacuna Atlas entry on it begins: “The elongated skulls of Paracas are real. So is the paperwork gap around where some of the burials went.” The same entry says: “Shaping an infant's skull with boards and bindings was a deliberate cultural practice, not a mystery of biology.”
      
        
      Paracas in the Lacuna Atlas · The Paracas bundles dataset
      
        
      Machu Picchu is real and much later. It was built in the mid-1400s as an estate for the Inca emperor Pachacuti, with terraces, fitted stone and trapezoid doorways. The game puts it on the same road as Paracas. Smarthistory: Machu Picchu
      
      
      

      
      
        
      What the jaguar borrows
      
        
      The jaguar's side of the game takes ideas from several ancient American traditions. They belong to different peoples and centuries, and the game mixes them freely.
      
        
        
          
      • The half form and the standing stones. Olmec carvings from Mexico show a face with a cleft head, slanted almond eyes and a downturned open mouth, known as the were-jaguar. Researchers disagree about what it shows, and some link it to an Olmec rain deity. The game shows a half form at each change, carves the cleft head on its standing stones, and has the roar bring rain. Wikipedia: Werejaguar
        • 
          
      • The double. In Mesoamerican belief a nagual is a guardian spirit that lives in an animal such as a deer, a jaguar or a bird. In some places it is the animal a powerful person can turn into. The game leaves a double behind when you change. Britannica: nagual
        • 
          
      • The roar. The Aztec god Tepeyollotl, whose name means heart of the mountain, is a jaguar tied to caves, echoes and earthquakes. Wikipedia: Tepeyollotl
        • 
          
      • The paths only the jaguar walks. Maya art has a jaguar god that scholars have read as the night sun: the sun on its way through the underworld. Wikipedia: Maya jaguar gods
        • 
          
      • The drink. At Chavín de Huántar in the Peruvian highlands, carvings around the Circular Plaza show a figure with feline and human traits holding a San Pedro cactus. The cactus is thought to be the source of the drug used in rituals there. Stanford: Chavín de Huántar
        • 
        
      
      
      

      
      
        
      Limits
      
        
        
          
      • The touch controls are checked in Chrome's phone emulation, sideways and upright, and the game has been opened on one iPhone. It has not been played through on a phone or tablet.
        • 
          
      • If you choose to play upright on a phone, the picture is small and the words drawn inside the last stage's picture are hard to read.
        • 
          
      • On an iPhone the game asks to play its sound even when the ringer switch is set to silent, which stops other apps' audio while the sound is on. This has not been checked on a phone. Before iOS 16.4 the ringer switch silences the game.
        • 
          
      • The story pace has no touch control. On a phone it is set to 220 words a minute.
        • 
          
      • Checked in Chrome on a desktop. Firefox and Safari have not been checked.
        • 
          
      • Each of the first five stages has two scripted runs that play it to the exit: one that takes the jaguar's form and one that never does. A third check plays the last stage and confirms that it ends without an exit.
        • 
          
      • THE ANOMALOUS SYSTEM is a first version. One scripted run plays it to its exit, using both forms. It has no run that stays in one form, because a copy is left by changing form. It has been checked in headless Chrome at a desktop width and a phone width. No person has played it through yet, and it has not been opened on a phone.
        • 
          
      • The ending covers the whole page and changes this tab's title for about ten seconds. Enter or Escape skips it at any point. Once the page goes dark for the last four lines, a tap skips the rest. With reduced motion set on your device it shows its text and no animation.
        • 
          
      • The ending reads the time from your device's clock and shows it to you. The game sends nothing: the only requests it makes are for its own files on this site, so what it reads stays on your device.
        • 
          
      • Your progress, the Atlas marks you have found, your story pace and your best share in THE ANOMALOUS SYSTEM are kept in this browser's storage. Clearing site data resets them.
        • 
          
      • The music and the effects are synthesised in the browser. Their level was set by measurement in Chrome, without listening.
