The game

PSYCHO SYBER: ARENA is a first-person game in 3D that runs in this page. It has two modes. Waves is for one player. The Duel is for two players in two browsers. It is a second game beside PSYCHO SYBER, the side-scrolling stealth game, and it uses the same two forms.

The arena, its enemies and the Kernel are invented. SyberLabs makes software for checking AI's work: SyberWork, Attest, RISE and FLYSPACE. Lacuna Institute is its research institute.

Two forms

Q changes your form. Each change sends out a pulse that damages enemies within reach, erases enemy shots near you and makes your dash and ability ready again. For three seconds after a change your hits do half as much damage again.

SYBER
Fires an arc rifle. A shot that hits an enemy jumps to one more enemy nearby and tags both for six seconds. The ability throws a grapple that pulls you toward the block or enemy it hits. Shift blinks you a short way in the direction you are looking, twice before a wait. Space against a wall, in the air, kicks you off it.
PSYCHO
Fires rockets. A rocket breaks the blocks around the place it lands, and one fired at your own feet throws you upward without hurting you. Space jumps a second time in the air. Shift rams forward through blocks and enemies. The ability, on the ground, launches you upward; in the air it slams you down, and the landing is a blast.

The two forms work together in four ways.

  • Any Psycho blast sets off every tag at once.
  • A Psycho blast throws the lighter enemies into the air, and the arc rifle does double damage to an enemy in the air.
  • Changing form while the grapple is attached drops you on its anchor, and the landing is a blast larger than a slam's.
  • Kills fill a sync meter, drawn as rings on the floor. A kill made in a different form from the kill before it fills the meter faster. When it is full, the next change of form makes you Psycho Syber for nine seconds: both weapons fire and the dash and ability have no wait.

Blocks that lose their support fall, and a falling block damages the enemies it lands on.

Waves

Enemies arrive in waves. Each wave has two more enemies than the one before, up to 32. Every fifth wave brings the Kernel, a large enemy with a shield, and half as many others. From the third wave some enemies carry a shield in one of two colours: a rose shield breaks only to Psycho and a teal shield only to Syber. The arena is rebuilt after each wave and you get back 25 health.

After each wave you choose one of three operators, drawn from five: Stochastic, Dynamical, Generative, Geometric and Experiential. An operator is an upgrade that lasts until the game ends, and each has three levels. The card for each one says what it changes.

Waves sends nothing: the only requests it makes are for its own files on this site. Your best score and your aim speed are kept in this browser's storage.

The Duel

Two players meet on a map that is the same from both ends. One is Psycho and the other is Syber. You cannot change form during a round, so a duel has no tags to set off and no sync meter. The forms trade every round. A round ends when one player's health runs out. The first to win five rounds wins, and either player can call a rematch.

A duel does not pause. P or Esc opens the panel: while it is open you stand still and your rival can still hit you, and the next round closes it.

Starting one

  1. The first player presses Duel, then Start a duel. The page shows a code of six letters and digits, and a link that carries it.
  2. They send the code or the link to the second player, by any message app or aloud.
  3. The second player opens the link and presses Join the duel, or presses Duel on this page and types the code. The first round starts in both browsers.

A code is for one duel. A duel that nobody joins is closed after ten minutes, and any duel is closed 45 minutes after it was started. If your connection drops, the page tries for twelve seconds to take your place back; after that your rival is told that you left.

What is sent

In a duel your browser keeps one connection open to a relay at syberlabs.io/duel, a program SyberLabs runs on Cloudflare, which also serves this site. The two browsers do not connect to each other, and your rival's browser is not given your network address. Nothing is contacted until you press Start a duel or Join.

  • Your browser sends the relay your position and aim 30 times a second, your shots, the hits you report and the blocks you break. The relay passes them to your rival's browser.
  • The relay keeps the countdown, both players' health, the rounds and the score while the duel lasts.
  • The relay writes no log and stores nothing. When both players have left, the duel is gone.
  • To limit abuse, the relay counts for one minute at a time how many duels are started and joined from one network address, and refuses more than 6 new duels or 30 joins.
  • The code is all that lets someone into your duel. Anyone who has it can take the second place, so send it only to the person you are playing.

Limits of the Duel

  • Each browser reports its own hits. The relay counts hits only while a round is live and caps the damage of one hit and of one second of hits. It does not check aim, so a player who alters the game can still cheat within those caps.
  • What you see of your rival is a tenth of a second old, plus the delay of both players' connections to the relay.
  • A script has played the Duel between two copies of headless Chrome, through a copy of the relay on the same machine: a duel to five rounds, a rematch, a connection that dropped and came back, and a player who left. Two people have not yet played it through the relay on Cloudflare, and it has not been tried on a phone or in Firefox or Safari.

On a phone or tablet

A game takes the whole screen, upright or sideways. The left thumb moves and the right thumb aims. Five buttons fire, jump, use the ability, dash and change form, and a drag that starts on a held button keeps aiming. Aim is pulled a little toward the nearest enemy. The pause button is at the top right; Leave the game, in the pause panel, brings back this page and its header.

Limits

  • The game needs WebGL. It draws with three.js r160, served from this site.
  • Checked in headless Chrome at a desktop width and a phone width. No person has played this version through yet, and Firefox and Safari have not been checked.
  • When frames slow down the game turns off its glow, and then lowers its resolution. Pause has a button for the glow.
  • With reduced motion set on your device, the picture behind the title holds still. A game in play moves as usual.
  • Sound is synthesised in the browser and starts when a game starts. Pause has a button to turn it off.
  • Two scripted checks run on every change to the site. One plays the relay's rules: rounds, health, the caps and dropped connections. The other checks that this page and the game's script agree. The checks that play a wave and a duel in a browser are run by hand.
