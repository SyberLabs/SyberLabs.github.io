Why a globe

No flat map can be true everywhere: a sphere can't be flattened without stretching it, which Gauss proved in 1827. So the default view is the globe itself, which shows true shapes at the centre of view.

The flat view uses Equal Earth (Šavrič, Patterson & Jenny, 2018). It keeps every region's true area, so Africa and the Amazon aren't shrunk next to Europe as they are on Mercator.

    How the past is drawn

    Past coastlines come from today's land and seafloor heights, flooded to the global sea level of each moment after Lambeck et al. 2014 (PNAS). Land that has risen or sunk since then is not corrected for, so treat the edges as approximate.

    Ice sheets are simplified outlines after Dyke 2004 (North America) and Hughes et al. 2016, DATED-1 (Eurasia), shrunk along their dated retreat. They are sketches, not survey lines.

    The Green Sahara follows the timing in Tierney, Pausata & deMenocal 2017 (Science Advances), and Lake Mega-Chad the shorelines in Drake & Bristow 2006 (The Holocene). Their extents are schematic.

    Why 25,000 years: across that span the continents sat where they are now, so what changed the human world was ice and water. Terrain: Mapzen terrain tiles on AWS Open Data (ETOPO1, GEBCO, SRTM and others).
