# SyberLabs homepage

Company site for [syberlabs.io](https://syberlabs.io/).

## How it is built

Static pages on Cloudflare Pages, built with Vite. `pnpm install` and `pnpm build:site` write the whole site to `dist/`;
`pnpm dev` serves it for work on the homepage and the shared styles.

- **Homepage.** `src/home/page.js` renders the page to HTML at build time from the parts in `src/home/parts/` (Vite
  writes it into `index.html`), so it is complete without JavaScript. `src/home/main.js` mounts the Line in the first
  screen (`src/visuals/line.js`; its rules are `src/visuals/line-sim.js`, tested by
  `node --test src/visuals/line-sim.test.mjs`) and fetches the rest when it scrolls near: the recorded run's
  behaviour, the Lacuna globe, the product marks, the four pieces and the field behind the contact part.
  `src/home/home.css` styles it. The page ships no framework.
- **Shared stylesheet.** `/syberlabs.css` is `fonts/site.css` followed by `src/styles/` (`tokens`, `base`, `type`,
  `chrome`, `components`). The Midnight theme is defined once, in `tokens.css`. Each static page links it and, where it
  needs one, its own small stylesheet.
- **Shared script.** `/syberlabs.js` (`src/site/`) adds the Menu's keyboard and focus behaviour and a back-to-top button.
  The Menu is the full-screen site map behind the header's Menu button (also `/` on the keyboard). It is a `<details>`,
  so it opens and closes without JavaScript, marks the current page in its markup, and lists the products, the
  company's services, research, contact, subscriber and privacy destinations; the footer keeps contact, services,
  GitHub, subscriber, staff and privacy access in one small bar.
- **Header and footer.** `projects/project-template.js` holds the markup. `node scripts/site-chrome.mjs` writes it into
  the static pages; CI runs it with `--check`, so a page whose chrome has drifted fails the build. Navigation and
  product data live in `projects/site-data.js`.
- **Fonts.** Every typeface is a file in `fonts/` (`fonts/README.md`). No page loads anything from another origin.
- **Cache.** `scripts/stamp.mjs` adds each local stylesheet's and script's content hash to its links in `dist/`, and
  `_headers` lets browsers keep `/assets/` and `/fonts/v1/` for a year.
- **Checks.** `node scripts/qa.mjs` sweeps the built site (links, headings, metadata, the banned word).
  `node scripts/shoot.mjs <dir>` screenshots every page at desktop and phone widths (headless Chromium). Every page
  carries Open Graph and Twitter card tags pointing at a committed 1200×630 card in `og/`; `node scripts/og-cards.mjs`
  regenerates the cards after a headline, intro or status changes.

`kit/v2/` is SyberLabs Design System v2 "Atlas", the kit product apps vendor (`syber-atlas.css` tokens and components,
`syber-atmosphere.js` plate engine, `syber-sigil.js` sigils; see `kit/v2/README.md`). It is published at
[syberlabs.io/kit/v2/](https://syberlabs.io/kit/v2/), its canonical public home. The site uses its sigil and plate
scripts and has its own stylesheet. The public RISE demo is copied into `dist/rise-demo/` by the build, and
`omni-demo/` holds the OmniOS recording (the acceptance test's "morning" journey against a local build with fixture
data; see the caption on the OmniOS page).

The **System maps** components (`src/system-maps/`) draw Relay, FLYSPACE (formerly OmniOS), RISE, Barn, OSAHR and SyberRuntime from their own repositories. The RISE and FLYSPACE maps are mounted on their project pages as an **Inspect** section: `src/system-map-embed.js` is built unhashed as `/system-map.js`, a small loader that imports `src/system-map-mount.jsx` (React, the maps and their linked stylesheet) when the section nears the viewport and renders one map into `[data-system-map]`. They are the only pages that load React. The maps of the four retired systems are kept in the source and mounted nowhere. Recorded traces in `src/*-trace.json` are regenerated by the scripts in `scripts/`; the RISE map is rule-based from `src/core/decision/`; `docs/SYSTEM_MAPS.md` records the evidence, commits and grades for every map.

Four products are published: SyberWork, Attest, RISE and FLYSPACE (formerly OmniOS, at /projects/flyspace/; /projects/omnios/ redirects). Relay and OSAHR were retired from the product set on 2026-11-05; their old addresses, and other retired addresses, redirect to the homepage (`_redirects`). Each project page is rendered from `projects/site-data.js` through `projects/project-template.js`: a live bar when the thing runs somewhere (RISE at rise.syberlabs.io, the OmniOS preview at omni.syberlabs.io), an optional demo film, then Why, Use, Design, Evidence and Facts. The RISE page keeps its hand-authored hero and paced passage, and `scripts/prerender.mjs` fills its `<!-- rise:sections -->` marker with the same sections at build time. Every evidence row carries the state it has reached (implemented, tested, measured, deployed, not yet) and the page names the commit it reflects. Commons was removed on 2026-10-05; its old addresses redirect to the homepage (`_redirects`). OmniOS is deployed from its own repository (see its `DEPLOYMENT.md`); the earlier manual deploy workflow in this repository was removed with it.

The reader-owned AI status lives in `kev/` and is published at `/kev/`. The prior Jev evaluation and integration case study remains at `/jev/` as historical evidence; its results must not be attributed to Kev.


**Lacuna Institute** is SyberLabs' research institute, and its pages are part of this site since 2026-11-16: `lacuna/` is published as it is at [syberlabs.io/lacuna/](https://syberlabs.io/lacuna/) (`scripts/assemble-dist.sh` copies it). `lacuna/index.html` is the Lacuna Atlas, a globe of 21 research sites that unrolls into an Equal Earth map, with a time bar for the last 25,000 years; it is generated from `src/lacuna/atlas/` (template, site list, map and past-Earth data) by `python3 src/lacuna/atlas/build.py`, and the build fails when the committed page is stale. `python3 src/lacuna/atlas/share.py` writes one share page per site (`lacuna/sites/<slug>/`, a link preview that forwards to that site on the Atlas) and the 1200×630 cards in `lacuna/share/`. `lacuna/research/paracas-bundles/` is the Paracas bundles dataset page (plain HTML) with its CSV in `lacuna/data/`. The Atlas loads d3 7.9.0 and topojson-client 3.1.0 from `lacuna/vendor/` (ISC, licences beside them), so no page loads a third-party script. The pages moved here from the `lacunainstitute.org` site; that domain and `lacunainstitute.com` are short addresses for `/lacuna/`, redirected path for path by one rule in each Cloudflare zone (`src/lacuna/README.md` has the rule), and `lacunainstitute.org` remains the institute's email domain. Lacuna pages keep their own typography and colours and link to the homepage with "Part of SyberLabs"; the header (`Lacuna`), the Menu, the research index, `sitemap.xml`, `llms.txt` and the homepage's structured data list them. The homepage has a Lacuna section: `src/visuals/atlas.js` draws the Earth as dots with the Atlas's sites marked and names each site in turn as it comes round, and the globe links to the site named. `node scripts/lacuna-home.mjs` generates its data (`src/visuals/atlas-data.js`) from the Atlas's site list and land outline, and the build fails when that file is stale.

**PSYCHO SYBER** is a browser game published at [syberlabs.io/psycho-syber/](https://syberlabs.io/psycho-syber/) since 2026-11-16 (`psycho-syber/`, copied by `scripts/assemble-dist.sh`). It is a side-scrolling stealth game with two forms, changed with Shift, and six stages: one for each product, one for Lacuna Institute and a last one with no exit. The rules are `psycho-syber/game/sim.js`, which has no DOM, so `node psycho-syber/test/run.mjs` plays every stage that has an exit to it with a scripted run; CI does the same. `psycho-syber/README.md` describes the files, the map characters and how to add a stage.

The research index at `research/` (published at `/research/`) carries one card per research artifact, on this site and in the SyberLabs/papers, cross-platform and OSAHR_Cell repositories, with its claim, evidence state, limits, date and source; the `Reflects` line at its foot names the commits it was checked against, so update it when they move.

The Sybershoke research note lives at `research/sybershoke/`. Revision 2 reports the Week 0 harness against its reference simulator (that repository is still unpublished), the public [SyberLabs/sybershoke](https://github.com/SyberLabs/sybershoke) workspace, and its first real system, the RISE Worker source run against recorded Jev answers; every number on it comes from that project's committed reports (`REPORT.md`, `docs/REDTEAM.md`, `docs/ADAPTER-RISE.md`), so update the note when they change.

The GrokCell Execution report lives at `research/jev-execution/`. It summarizes the durable offline prototype and its evidence limits; preserve the distinction between observed results and planned live integration.
