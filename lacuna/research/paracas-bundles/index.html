Paracas bundles that left Peru

A census of the whole mummy bundles from the Paracas Necropolis of Wari Kayan that published sources show leaving Peru: where each went, who is thought to hold it now, and how well each claim is sourced.

The Necropolis of Wari Kayan, on the Paracas Peninsula, was excavated under Julio C. Tello from 1927, and its bundles carry Tello’s numbers. This version counts only whole bundles. It is built from published literature and museum catalogues. Where sources disagree, the table and notes say so rather than choosing between them.

Notes

Which six went to Seville

Six bundles were shipped on 10 January 1929 for Peru’s pavilion at the Exposición Iberoamericana. The two published lists of the six do not match. Daggett (1994: 57), citing Tello and Mejía Xesspe, gives Nos. 33, 54, 90, 155, 192 and 203. Villegas (2015: 154–155), citing Antonio Hurtado’s report of January 1929, gives Nos. 33, 38, 112, 115, 192 and one unnumbered bundle. Only 33 and 192 appear in both. Daggett (2016) records No. 38 being studied in Lima in November 1930, which weighs against its inclusion. The table follows Daggett 1994; the Villegas numbers are in the CSV as alternatives, not counted.

The collection was held in Seville after the exhibit closed in 1930. Tello asked for its return intact in October 1931. Carrión Cachot saw the exhibit abandoned in 1952, and Daggett (1994: 66) found no evidence that any of the bundles or their contents returned. Their present whereabouts are not established.

The 1937 bundles: loan or gift

Four bundles were chosen by Tello in June 1937 under a government decision to give them to the Metropolitan Museum of Art, which declined them. Three passed to the American Museum of Natural History and one to Harvard’s Peabody Museum. Contemporary wording in 1938 describes them as lent by the Peruvian government (Natural History 1938; Harvard Crimson, 29 March 1938). Later records describe them as a gift of Nelson A. Rockefeller (Towle 1952; Daggett 1994: 61). The sources read do not record a transfer of title. The Harvard bundle is numbered 16 in Daggett 1994 and 6 in Daggett 2021, both citing the same page of Tello and Mejía Xesspe (1967: 215).

Bundle 49 and bundle 294

No. 49 travelled to New York in August 1949 to be opened for the 29th International Congress of Americanists by Rebeca Carrión Cachot and Junius Bird, and returned to Peru on 4 January 1950. No. 294 was given to El Salvador and opened in San Salvador on 10 July 1956, according to Carrión Cachot’s own report. A Peruvian-donated Paracas mummy is recorded in storage at the national museum (MUNA) in 2013, without a bundle number; that it is No. 294 is an inference.

Not counted

  • Museo de América, Madrid, inv. 70311. A whole bundle catalogued as from Wari Kayan, with no Tello number or acquisition history. Its link to Seville rests on unsourced claims, so it is listed as a candidate only.
  • Bundle contents abroad. Textiles from one bundle at the Gothenburg museum (returned from 2014), a textile given to Herbert Hoover in 1928, and a mantle from bundle 421 (returned 2014–15) are in the CSV for reference. They are not whole bundles.

Methods and sources

Each row records one bundle, with its departure, route, first recipient, current holder, opening, catalogue numbers, photographs, the wording of loan or gift, return, and any conflicts between sources. Every claim in the CSV carries a grade (P, R or W) beside its source. A holder is marked presumed when it is inferred from an older record and no current record was seen. Nothing has been inferred beyond what the sources state, and where an inference is noted it is labelled as one.

Limits of this version: parts of Daggett 2016 were consulted through a summary and have not yet been checked against the original; Daggett 2021 was consulted in part; and the text of Iriarte 2004 has not been read. The online collection database of the American Museum of Natural History was unavailable when checked in November 2026. A future version will add the bundles held in Lima, so that the exported bundles can be compared with the whole collection.
