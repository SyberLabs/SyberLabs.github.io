Historical notes

Which six went to Seville (1929)

Six bundles shipped 10 January 1929 for Peru’s pavilion at the Exposición Iberoamericana. Published lists conflict: Daggett (1994: 57, citing Tello and Mejía Xesspe) lists Nos. 33, 54, 90, 155, 192, and 203; Villegas (2015: 154–155, citing Antonio Hurtado’s January 1929 report) lists Nos. 33, 38, 112, 115, 192, and one unnumbered bundle. Only Nos. 33 and 192 overlap, and Daggett (2016) records No. 38 under study in Lima in November 1930. The table follows Daggett 1994 and logs Villegas’s alternatives in the CSV.

Tello requested their intact return in October 1931; Carrión Cachot observed the exhibit abandoned in 1952, and Daggett (1994: 66) found no evidence of return. Current whereabouts remain unestablished.

The 1937 bundles: loan or gift

Selected by Tello in June 1937 for the Metropolitan Museum of Art (which declined them), three passed to the American Museum of Natural History and one to Harvard’s Peabody Museum. Contemporary 1938 reports describe a Peruvian government loan (Natural History 1938; Harvard Crimson, 29 March 1938); later texts cite a gift of Nelson A. Rockefeller (Towle 1952; Daggett 1994: 61). Consulted records show no transfer of title. Daggett numbers the Harvard bundle 16 (1994) and 6 (2021) from the same source page (Tello & Mejía Xesspe 1967: 215).

Bundles 49 and 294

No. 49 traveled to New York in August 1949 for opening at the 29th International Congress of Americanists by Rebeca Carrión Cachot and Junius Bird, returning to Peru on 4 January 1950. No. 294 was gifted to El Salvador and opened 10 July 1956 per Carrión Cachot’s report; a 2013 record places an unnumbered Peruvian-donated Paracas mummy in storage at MUNA (San Salvador), inferred but unconfirmed as No. 294.

Excluded from main count

  • Museo de América, Madrid, inv. 70311. A whole Wari Kayan bundle lacking Tello numbering or acquisition history; its Seville link rests on unsourced claims and is flagged candidate-only.
  • Bundle contents abroad. Exported textiles without whole bundles (Gothenburg, returned from 2014; Herbert Hoover 1928; bundle 421 mantle, returned 2014–15) appear in the CSV for reference only.

Methods and limits

Each row logs departure, route, recipient, current holder, opening date, catalogue numbers, photos, loan/gift wording, return status, and source conflicts. Every claim carries a P, R, or W grade; inferences are labeled explicitly.

Version 1.0 limits: parts of Daggett (2016) rely on summaries pending direct check; Daggett (2021) was consulted partially; Iriarte (2004) full text was unread; AMNH’s online database was unreachable in November 2026. Future versions will add bundles remaining in Lima.
